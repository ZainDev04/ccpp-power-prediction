How it was built

Public data from a real plant: hourly averages recorded over six years while it ran at full load. The operators never changed the set point, yet output moved between 420 and 496 MW with the weather.

  1. Audit the raw data

    9,568 hours. The file held 41 duplicate rows, 55 humidity readings above 100%, and 182 hours where the vacuum reads higher than the air pressure, so its unit label cannot be right.

  2. Clean it with fixed rules

    Duplicates dropped and humidity capped at 100%. Unusual weather was kept, because it is real and it is where predictions matter. 9,527 hours remain.

  3. Lock away a test set

    7,621 hours for training and 1,906 for the final test, split so both hold the same mix of low and high output. Scaling is fitted on training hours only.

  4. Tune three models

    Support vector regression, k-nearest neighbours and a neural network, each tuned with 5-fold cross-validation on the training hours.

  5. Test once and choose

    KNN was the most accurate and made the fewest costly over-predictions: more than 5 MW too high on 5.3% of hours, against 6.9% and 7.8% for the others.

Results on 1,906 unseen hours

All three models beat the best published result on this dataset (R² 0.912). The comparison is indicative, because the published study used a different split.
